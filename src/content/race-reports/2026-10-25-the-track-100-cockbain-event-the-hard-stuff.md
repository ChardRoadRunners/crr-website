Whilst most sensible people were eyeing up a cosy weekend indoors from storm Boelo and looking forward to an extra hour in bed when the clocks went back, I got up at 0230 and drove to Huntington Outdoor Athletics Track, Cambridgeshire to take on this inconceivable task! Nabbing less than 2 hours sleep in the car park pre the race brief I wondered onto the track with my box of spare clothes, x4 chicken tikka slices, a box of flap jacks and 8 bottles of raspberry lucozade .😬 Eyeing up the other competitors I quickly realised that I was amongst potential Spartathlon Qualifiers and those preparing for an easy pre Jogle! Each one of them had a trolley with drawers of spare clothes, multiple trainers, pre mixed bottles, chairs and regular food intake!

44 competitors ready to go on the start line. The race brief was simple - no headphones, keep running until you are informed of your last lap and halfway through you will change direction 🤔

Off we went. My plan - do my own thing and ignore what the others were doing! 

Laps were steady in the beginning but knowing that bad weather was inbound I knew I wanted to give myself breathing room so I pushed on, barely stopping up to marathon distance and beyond.  My first wobble came at 32 miles   - the wind had picked up down one side of the track and everybody was leaning hard into it making superman poses to keep balance. Everything I was wearing was soaked from sweat and rain. After the first 2 hours of heavy rain my trainers were squelching, the waterproofs were no longer waterproof and the first aches and hotspots were niggling! We lost a few runners who called it a day and to be honest if it had been closer to home I think I would have called it!!  But I didn’t. I pushed on and did another lap, then another and another……. Hours in the wind and rain - I’m not going to lie – it seemed endless and I got into a routine of jog a few laps, walk a lap, take on fluid & a small bite of food whilst emptying the small puddle of water that kept forming at the end of my waterproof gloves. 😜 

The rain stopped and I spent 20 minutes stripping off my wet things, drying off, and treating myself to a set of dry socks and clothes. Felt really good!! One and a half laps later the heavens opened and once again I found myself completely drenched - typical! It did eventually stop and the clouds cleared to a bright moon which was nice to see. The wind chill in the night dropped to -1 degrees - joggers, softy jacket, woolly hat and gloves became a balancing act of staying warm or overheating - more unpleasant hours especially as the hands started swelling! With 35 miles still to go it was a battle of overcoming aches and pains, self treating and drawing on experiences and having harsh words that you will do this if any one can…! More runners dropped out or vastly slowed up; it was admirable to see the personal battles unfolding in close proximity! 

9 miles from the end I struggled to work out timings to see myself comfortably in the cut off - I was happy with completion rather than pushing for a time as that went out the window with the weather. 7 miles to go and any distraction helped - I found through tired eyes I was making faces out of the lichen on the track floor - particularly just after each lap there was one patch that looked like Michael Jackson and E.T 😂 

13 finishers crossed the line within the time limit - myself 24hrs 14 min!! 

It has taken a long few days to recover and write this! I’m pleased now that not only did I achieve the Track 100 but to do it through that weather was completely bonkers and I’m never going to forget it!
